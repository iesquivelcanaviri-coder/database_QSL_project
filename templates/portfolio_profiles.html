{# ------------------------------------------------------------ #} {# Benchmark and Portfolio Rules Subsection #} {# ------------------------------------------------------------ #}

3. Benchmark and Portfolio Rules

Client’s reference market/index. The app uses this ticker to compare each selected stock or ETF against the client’s portfolio benchmark and to calculate beta. Example: use ACWI for a global equity client, SPY for a US equity client, or IEF/SHY for a bond-focused client.

Client’s benchmark written in normal language for the saved profile. This should match the benchmark ticker above. Example: if the ticker is ACWI, choose MSCI ACWI; if the client has a balanced mandate, choose 40/60 global portfolio.

Client’s maximum allocation to one individual holding. This helps show concentration risk. Example: conservative or corporate clients may use 5%; diversified private clients may use 10%; aggressive clients may allow 15% or 20%.

Client’s base currency and whether foreign-currency exposure is acceptable. Example: choose EUR only if the client wants no FX exposure; choose EUR base, FX allowed if the client reports in EUR but can hold USD/global assets.

Client’s product or mandate restriction. Use this to record what the portfolio manager should respect when selecting investments. Example: UCITS for European-regulated funds, Corporate policy prohibits equities for treasury clients, or None if there is no special restriction.

Client’s sustainability preference or exclusion rule. Example: Required means ESG must be considered; No tobacco means tobacco-related holdings should be avoided; Neutral means no specific ESG preference.

{# ------------------------------------------------------------ #} {# Liquidity and Financial Context Subsection #} {# ------------------------------------------------------------ #}

4. Liquidity and Financial Context

Client’s approximate total wealth across cash, property, investments, and other assets. This helps the portfolio manager judge whether the client can absorb losses.

Client’s existing investments outside this specific mandate. This helps avoid over-concentration if the client already has exposure elsewhere.

Client’s approximate debts, loans, mortgages, or financial obligations. Higher liabilities normally reduce the client’s capacity to take investment risk.

Client’s need for cash access. Choose Low if the client can stay invested for longer; Medium if some cash may be needed; High or Monthly liquidity required if the portfolio must remain easy to sell or income/cash is needed regularly.

How the portfolio should generally be managed.

Helps assess whether the portfolio could cause stress in downturns.
